## Grasserie (nuclear polyhedrosis virus, NPV)
Symptoms: larvae become restless and climb to the edge of the tray, skin turns shiny, swollen and fragile, milky fluid oozes out when ruptured; usually appears in the later instars (4th-5th). Spread: contaminated litter, rearing equipment, handling. Mitigation: immediately remove and burn infected larvae, do not throw them in the open, disinfect rearing house, trays and equipment with a bleaching powder or formalin solution, maintain proper temperature and humidity, avoid overcrowding and poor-quality leaf. Highly infectious: escalate to the reeler if many trays are affected, because cocoon loss can be large.

## Flacherie
Symptoms: larvae are sluggish, stop feeding, body becomes flaccid, vomiting and diarrhoea, blackening and foul smell after death. Causes: bacteria or virus favoured by poor-quality or wet leaf, high humidity and temperature fluctuations. Mitigation: feed fresh, good-quality dry leaf, improve ventilation, regular bed cleaning, remove sick larvae, disinfect between batches.

## Muscardine (white or green)
Cause: fungus (Beauveria bassiana). Symptoms: dead larvae become hard, mummified and covered with white chalky powder. Spread by spores. Mitigation: remove and destroy mummified larvae, use a recommended bed disinfectant (lime or dithane-type dusting), reduce humidity and increase ventilation, disinfect rearing house before next batch.

## Pebrine
Cause: microsporidian protozoan (Nosema bombycis). Symptoms: uneven growth, small pepper-like black spots on skin, poor cocoons. Transmitted from mother moth to eggs. Mitigation: use only certified disease-free layings, examine mother moths, destroy infected batches, strictly sanitise. This is a serious notifiable problem; escalate immediately and inform the reeler.

## Healthy larvae
Healthy larvae are active, feed well, have a firm body with clear skin colour for the instar and produce uniform dry droppings.
