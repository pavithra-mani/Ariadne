# Mulberry stem canker and root knot (working notes)

Source: general knowledge, review by expert. Not a citation of a published document; replace with a scraped CSB / university extension page when available.

## Stem canker
- Looks like: sunken, cracked or discoloured patches on bark of stems and branches; shoots above the patch may wilt or die back.
- Spreads through pruning wounds, contaminated cutting tools and infected cuttings.
- Management: prune cankered stems well below the lesion and burn or bury the cuttings; disinfect tools between plants; avoid wounding stems; paint large pruning cuts; use disease-free cuttings for new plantings.
- Leaf quality on affected branches falls, so do not feed visibly diseased branches' leaves to silkworms until an expert has checked them.

## Root knot (nematode)
- Looks like: yellowing, stunted plants that wilt in the heat of the day; roots carry small swellings (galls).
- Spreads through infested soil, water and planting material.
- Management: use nematode-free saplings; rotate or fallow infested plots; add well-decomposed organic manure; remove and destroy heavily galled plants; ask the extension officer about approved nematicides or bio-agents.
- Expect gradual yield loss over seasons rather than a sudden drop.

## When to escalate
If more than a fifth of the plot shows symptoms or the cause is unclear, ask a sericulture extension officer to inspect before harvesting leaf for rearing.
