## Reeling process
Stifled cocoons are sorted, cooked or boiled to soften the sericin, brushed to find the filament ends, and reeled on a reeling machine into raw silk yarn (hanks or bales). Roughly eight to twelve kilograms of fresh cocoons are needed for one kilogram of raw silk, depending on breed, cocoon quality and reeling efficiency.

## Factors affecting low yarn output
Poor cocoon quality (high pierced or double cocoon share), irregular cocoon supply, machine breakdown, power cuts, incorrect basin water temperature and unskilled operators. Check cocoon stock and quality first, then machine and utilities.

## Raising falling yarn stock
Prioritise supply to customers with the lowest remaining stock cover, increase reeling hours within capacity, source alternative cocoons from nearby farmers or other reelers, and tell affected spinners early with a revised delivery date.

## Yarn storage
Store raw silk in a clean dry room at moderate humidity, away from direct sunlight, pests and strong odours. Protect hanks and bales from moisture which causes mildew and weakens the yarn.

## Delivery delays
When a delivery is delayed, notify the spinner with an honest estimate. If the delay exceeds the spinner's stock cover days, re-allocate remaining yarn by urgency and find an alternative source for the shortfall.
